# Sports Science Pacing Methodology & Training Zones

## 1. Jack Daniels VDOT & Lactate Threshold Fundamentals
- **Lactate Threshold (LT2):** The running velocity at which blood lactate accumulation exceeds systemic clearance (typically ~4.0 mmol/L). It represents the maximum steady-state intensity sustainable for 45 to 60 minutes in trained runners.
- **Goal of Threshold Workouts:** Expand the mitochondrial enzyme capacity and monocarboxylate transporter (MCT) density to shift the lactate curve to the right, allowing faster paces at lower physiological cost.

---

## 2. Pacing Zones Defined

### Zone 1: Active Recovery (< 68% HRmax)
- **Purpose:** Circulatory flush, glycogen replenishment, zero neuromuscular fatigue.
- **Pace:** Conversational; 90–120 seconds slower than 5K pace.
- **Breathing:** Pure nasal breathing or effortless conversational sentences.

### Zone 2: Aerobic Base / Easy (68–78% HRmax)
- **Purpose:** Mitochondrial biogenesis, capillary capillary density expansion, lipid oxidation.
- **Pace:** 60–90 seconds slower than 5K pace.
- **Duration:** 45 to 90 minutes.

### Zone 3: Steady / Marathon Pace (79–87% HRmax)
- **Purpose:** Glycogen utilization economy, sustained steady-state turnover.
- **Pace:** 30–45 seconds slower than 5K pace.

### Zone 4: Threshold / Cruise Intervals (88–92% HRmax)
- **Purpose:** Lactate clearance adaptation without entering anaerobic acidosis.
- **Interval Format:** 800m to 3,000m repetitions (e.g., 6x1km or 3x2km) with short active jog recoveries (typically 60–90 seconds, ~1:4 to 1:5 work-to-rest ratio).
- **Rule of Thumb:** Never sprint the final 200m; finish each repetition feeling capable of executing one additional rep with identical biomechanics.

### Zone 5: VO2 Max / Speed (93–98% HRmax)
- **Purpose:** Maximum cardiac output, stroke volume, and neuromuscular recruitment.
- **Format:** 400m to 1,200m intervals at 3K–5K race pace with equal or 1:1 work-to-rest ratios.

## 3. The "First Rep Brake" & Negative Splitting
1. **The Biological Trap:** Cold muscles and excess adrenaline cause runners to over-stride and surge on Rep 1, flooding fast-twitch muscle fibers with lactate and prematurely exhausting glycogen stores.
2. **The Execution Rule:** Rep 1 must be run at the slowest target pace boundary. 
3. **Negative Split Requirement:** Reps 2 through $N-1$ lock into exact target pace. The final rep may increase pace only within the final 25% of the distance if form and cadence remain pristine.

---

## 4. Aerobic Decoupling & Cardiac Drift
- **Formula:** Pw:HR ratio (Power/Pace vs Heart Rate between first half and second half of a steady workout).
- **Target:** Aerobic decoupling $< 5\%$.
- **Interpretation:** If heart rate drifts $> 5\%$ at constant pace, it signals either dehydration, glycogen depletion, poor aerobic base, or excessive heat stress.
