# Sports Science Pacing Methodology & Training Zones

## 1. Jack Daniels VDOT, Bakken Sub-Threshold & Critical Velocity Fundamentals
- **Lactate Threshold (`LT2` / `T` Pace):** The running velocity at which blood lactate production matches maximal systemic clearance (typically ~2.5–3.5 mmol/L in sub-threshold control, sustainable for ~60 minutes / ~15K–Half Marathon race effort). It is **slower** than 10K race pace for most runners.
- **Critical Velocity (`CV` / 10K Race Pace):** The physiological boundary (~90–94% HRmax, ~35–40 minute race effort) sitting between true `LT2` and `VO2max`. Running `CV` intervals requires longer recoveries (90–120s) than `LT2` cruise intervals due to net lactate accumulation.
- **Goal of Sub-Threshold Workouts:** Maximize high-quality volume at ~3.0 mmol/L blood lactate to expand mitochondrial enzyme capacity and monocarboxylate transporter (MCT1/MCT4) density while minimizing autonomic and musculoskeletal fatigue.

## 2. Six-Tier Pacing & Recovery Taxonomy

| Zone / Code | Physiological System | % HRmax | % VDOT | Interval Recovery Protocol | Primary Adaptation |
| :--- | :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Zone 1 (`Rec`)** | Active Recovery | `< 68%` | `55–63%` | Continuous | Parasympathetic restoration, capillary flush, zero tendon strain |
| **Zone 2 (`E`)** | Aerobic Base / Easy | `68–78%` | `65–74%` | Continuous | Mitochondrial biogenesis, lipid oxidation, musculoskeletal durability |
| **Zone 3 (`S` / `M`)** | Steady / Marathon Pace | `79–85%` | `78–83%` | Continuous | Glycogen utilization economy (avoid "gray zone" drift on easy days) |
| **Zone 4a (`T` / `LT2`)** | **Sub-Threshold / LT2** *(~60-min effort)* | `86–90%` | `86–89%` | **45–60s Passive Stand or Slow Walk** | Maximal Lactate Steady State (~3.0 mmol/L), lactate shuttle expansion |
| **Zone 4b (`CV` / `RP`)** | **Critical Velocity / 10K Pace** *(~40-min effort)* | `90–94%` | `91–94%` | **90–120s Easy Walk/Jog** | Specific 10K pace durability, H+ buffering capacity |
| **Zone 5 (`I` / `R`)** | **VO2 Max & Repetition** *(3K–5K / Strides)* | `95–100%` | `95–110%` | **2:00–2:30 Easy Jog** *(Full rest for strides)* | Stroke volume ceiling, maximal aerobic power, neuromuscular stiffness |

## 3. Workout-Specific Recovery Mechanics (Passive vs. Active Clearance)
1. **Why Short Passive/Walking Rest Wins for Sub-Threshold (`LT2`):**
   - During `LT2` cruise intervals (e.g., `5×1.2km` or `3×2km` at 60-minute effort), blood lactate remains modest (~2.5–3.5 mmol/L).
   - Forcing an athlete to jog continuously (`6:30/km`) during a short 60-second rest keeps heart rate pinned above 145 bpm, causing artificial cardiac drift ("recovery creep") and adding unnecessary eccentric impact to the Achilles.
   - **Rule:** Prescribe **45–60s standing or slow walking rest** for `LT2` cruise intervals to maximize rapid phosphocreatine (PCr) resynthesis and drop HR by 25–35 bpm between reps.
2. **When Active Jogging Recovery Is Required (`CV` & `VO2max`):**
   - During supra-threshold `CV` (`10K pace`) or `VO2max` (`5K/3K pace`) intervals, blood lactate exceeds 5–8 mmol/L.
   - **Rule:** Prescribe **90–150s easy walk/jog recovery** so skeletal muscle pumping accelerates venous return and lactate oxidation in oxidative Type I fibers.

## 4. The "First Rep Brake" & Grade-Adjusted Pacing
1. **The Biological Trap:** Adrenaline and fresh muscle glycogen tempt runners to surge on Rep 1, spiking glycolytic flux, depleting fast-twitch fibers, and triggering premature slow-component $\dot{V}O_2$ drift.
2. **The Execution Rule:** Rep 1 must be locked at the **slowest boundary** of the prescribed pace zone.
3. **Grade-Adjusted Intelligence:** Running uphill (`+10m to +25m` split gain) increases mechanical work linearly. Never penalize an athlete for slowing pace by 5–12s/km on an incline if heart rate and running power remain strictly within the target zone.

## 5. Aerobic Decoupling ($Pw:HR$) & Longitudinal Efficiency Factor ($\text{EF}$)
- **Aerobic Decoupling:** Compares Efficiency Factor ($\text{EF} = \frac{\text{Speed (m/min)}}{\text{Avg HR}}$) between the first half and second half of a steady run.
  - **Target:** Grade-adjusted decoupling $< 4.0\%$.
  - **Elevation Compensation:** Always correct for net elevation differentials between H1 and H2 before diagnosing cardiovascular fatigue.
- **Longitudinal Tracking:** Evaluate single-workout $\text{EF}$ against the athlete's 30-day rolling $\text{EF}$ trend to measure true aerobic remodeling over time.
