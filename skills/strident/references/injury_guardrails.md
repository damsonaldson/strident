# Biomechanical Safety & Tendinopathy Protocols

## 1. Tendon Loading Principles (Achilles & Patellar)
- Tendons adapt slower than muscle tissue because collagen fibers have lower vascularity.
- **The 24-Hour Latency Rule:** Tendon pain and stiffness typically peak 12 to 24 hours **after** the mechanical stress, not during the run.

---

## 2. Morning Stiffness Assessment (1–10 Scale)
Every morning, the athlete assesses Achilles/tendon status upon taking their first steps out of bed:
* **Score 0–2 (Green Light):** Negligible stiffness lasting $< 5$ minutes. Proceed with scheduled quality workout.
* **Score 3 (Yellow Light):** Mild stiffness dissipating within 15 minutes. Proceed with workout but mandate strict 2km progressive warmup and forbid spikes/low-drop shoes.
* **Score $\ge 4$ (Red Light):** Noticeable localized tenderness, crepitus, or stiffness lasting $> 20$ minutes. **Mandatory Intervention:** Cancel interval session immediately. Convert to flat Zone 1/2 recovery jog or non-impact cross-training (cycling/swimming).

---

## 3. Heavy Slow Resistance (HSR) Protocol
- **Exercise:** Standing calf raises (gastrocnemius focus) and seated calf raises (soleus focus).
- **Cadence:** 3 seconds concentric, 2 seconds isometric hold at peak, 3 seconds eccentric lowering.
- **Frequency:** 3 sessions per week (ideally performed on hard running days *after* the run to keep recovery days truly easy).
- **Loading:** 3–4 sets of 6–8 reps at ~70–80% 1RM.

---

## 4. Cadence & Stride Length Control
- Low cadence ($< 165$ spm) at threshold paces correlates strongly with over-striding, excessive braking forces, and high Achilles eccentric shock.
- Target cadence across all paces: **172–184 spm**.
- Emphasize pulling the foot up under the hip rather than reaching forward with the heel.
