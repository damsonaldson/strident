# Shoe Rotation & Heel-to-Toe Drop Strategy

## 1. Biomechanics of Heel-to-Toe Drop
- **Heel-to-Toe Drop (Offset):** The height difference between the heel cushion and the forefoot cushion.
- **High Drop (8–12 mm):** Shifts mechanical load **away from** the Achilles tendon and calf complex, redirecting stress toward the knee and quadriceps. Essential for runners managing posterior chain / Achilles sensitivity.
- **Low Drop (0–6 mm):** Promotes forefoot/midfoot strike and increases ankle dorsiflexion, transferring higher eccentric load onto the Achilles tendon and plantar fascia.

---

## 2. Recommended Shoe Rotation Framework

| Session Type | Recommended Drop | Cushioning | Plate Type | Objective |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Easy / Recovery** | 8 – 10 mm | High / Maximal | Non-plated | Total tendon unloading and joint protection |
| **Long Aerobic Run** | 8 – 10 mm | Moderate / High | Nylon / Unplated | Fatigue protection over prolonged footstrikes |
| **Threshold Intervals** | 8 mm | High energy return | Nylon / PEBA | Snappy turnover while maintaining heel protection |
| **Race Day / PR Test** | 6 – 8 mm | Superfoam (PEBA) | Full Carbon Plate | Maximum energy return and propulsive stiffness |

---

## 3. Strict Rules
1. Never wear brand new carbon-plated race shoes on back-to-back days.
2. If morning tendon stiffness $\ge 3/10$, ban all shoes with drop $< 8\text{ mm}$.
3. Retire shoes when foam resilience declines (typically 500–700 km depending on body weight and foam compound).
